# Website SEO — built 27 Nov 2026 (local only, nothing deployed)

## First, the honest part

Nobody can promise a #1 ranking, and anyone who does is selling something.
Ranking is decided by Google against every other cab site competing for the
same words, and it moves over months, not days.

What *can* be done is make the site eligible — and right now it mostly is not.
Before this work the site had **9 pages, no sitemap, no robots.txt, no
per-page titles and no structured data**. A search for "jodhpur to jaipur cab"
had nothing on this site to match: there was no page about that trip.

That is what this work fixes. It does not buy a ranking; it makes one possible.

---

## What now exists — the whole country

Coverage is **278 cities across all 35 states and union territories**, not the
10 the operator has switched on. The list is ported from
`mobile/lib/core/constants/indian_cities.dart`, the same one the app's city
picker already uses, so the site and the app agree on where the company will
drive instead of each keeping its own idea.

That makes **77,006 possible city-to-city routes**, and every one of them has a
working page.

| Page type | Live | Built ahead of time | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route | **77,006** | 400 | `/cabs/rajkot-to-panaji` |
| City | 278 | 278 | `/taxi/rajkot` |
| State | 35 | 35 | `/cab-service/goa` |
| Existing | 11 | 11 | `/`, `/pool`, `/support` |

### Why 400 route pages are built and not 77,006

Every pair works. A route nobody has asked for yet is rendered **on the first
request** — by a visitor or by a crawler — with its real distance fetched then
and cached afterwards. Verified: `/cabs/jorhat-to-silchar` and
`/cabs/bikaner-to-manali` were never prerendered, were requested cold, and came
back complete with the right title, canonical and real figures.

Building all 77,006 up front was rejected for two reasons, and the second
matters far more than the first:

1. **Cost and time.** Each page needs a live routing call for its real
   distance. Seventy-seven thousand of those on every deploy is a bill and an
   hours-long build, for pages most of which nobody will ever open.

2. **It would be read as a doorway.** Publishing tens of thousands of
   near-identical pages at once, from a site with no ranking history, is the
   exact pattern Google demotes — and the penalty lands on the **whole
   domain**, including the pages that were good. The cab sites that do carry
   route pages at this scale earned the authority first and grew into it.

So the 400 built ahead are the ones worth putting in front of a crawler on day
one: every route touching a city the operator actually serves, plus the routes
between the country's biggest cities. The rest are reachable through links on
their city pages and get indexed as they earn it.

### Served cities and the rest are not described the same way

Claiming hourly local hire in a town the company has never worked in would be a
lie, so the pages adapt:

| | Title |
|---|---|
| Jodhpur (admin list) | *Cab Service in Jodhpur — Outstation & Local Taxi Booking* |
| Rajkot (national list) | *Rajkot Cab Booking — Outstation Taxi & One Way Drop* |

### The route pages carry real data, not filler

A page per city pair is only worth publishing if each one says something true
and specific. These pull live figures from the same APIs the booking form uses:

- **road distance and drive time** from `/places/route` — Jodhpur→Jaipur reads
  334 km, 5h 48m, which is the driving distance, not a straight line
- **toll** for the route (₹1,090 on that example)
- **a fare table per car class** at the real per-km rate from the admin panel
- **five route-specific FAQs**

Everything is labelled "base fare" with toll, state tax, parking, driver
allowance and GST called out as extra — so the number on the SEO page cannot
contradict the number at checkout.

One scare during testing turned out to be my own: a stale `next start` from an
earlier build kept serving the old 10-city output on the same port and made the
new routes look like 404s. `pkill` does not kill Windows processes; the port had
to be cleared properly. Nothing was wrong with the code.

### What is deliberately kept out of search

`/cabs` (the fare-results screen), `/confirm`, `/booked` and `/book/...` are
`noindex, follow`. They render whatever trip is in the visitor's own browser,
so to a crawler they are empty shells.

They are **not** blocked in robots.txt, and that is on purpose: a blocked URL is
never fetched, so Google never sees the `noindex` on it and can still list the
bare address from a link. Allowing the crawl and serving `noindex` is what
actually keeps them out.

## Three things found along the way

**1. "rajkot to goa cab" now has a page — `/cabs/rajkot-to-panaji`, 1,189 km.**
Neither city is in the operator's admin list, which is exactly why the national
list was brought in: a person searching that is looking for a trip, not for a
list of cities an operator has switched on. Adding Rajkot in the admin panel
would upgrade it from a national city to a **served** one — stronger wording,
local hire offered, and every route touching it built ahead of time.

**2. Kota's state is saved as `"Raj"`, not `"Rajasthan"`.**
Left alone this would have minted a second state page at `/cab-service/raj`
competing with the real Rajasthan one — two thin pages for one place is exactly
what gets demoted. The code maps it back for now; the real fix is one edit in
the admin panel.

**3. The home page's city list is not in the server HTML.**
Measured: the rendered home page contains "Jaipur" **0 times** and "Ahmedabad"
**0 times**. Those chips are fetched by react-query after the page loads, so a
crawler sees an empty section. The new pages do not have this problem — they
are server components and their content is in the HTML. Worth fixing on the
home page later; it is not blocking.

## Next move, in order

### Yours, and they matter more than any code

1. **Promote the cities you actually operate in** to the admin list. Every city
   in India already has a page; adding one in the admin panel marks it
   **served**, which upgrades its wording, offers local hourly hire on it, and
   pulls every route touching it into the 400 built ahead of time and into the
   sitemap. This is now a priority decision, not a coverage one.
2. **Fix Kota's state** to "Rajasthan".
3. **Google Search Console** — verify `gorataxi.com`, submit
   `https://gorataxi.com/sitemap.xml`, then watch Coverage for pages that get
   excluded and why. This is the single highest-value step after deploying.
4. **Google Business Profile** — a cab company ranks locally on the map pack as
   much as on the blue links. Name, address, phone, hours, photos, and ask real
   customers for reviews.
5. **Backlinks** — local directories, travel listings, partner sites. This is
   what decides whether you outrank an established competitor, and it is the
   slowest part.

### Mine, when you say so

6. **Deploy** this to production (snapshot first, as always).
7. **Prefill the booking form from a route page** — right now "Check fare &
   book" lands on an empty form. Carrying the two cities through would lift
   conversions and the engagement signals Google reads.
8. **Server-render the home page's city and content sections.**
9. **Unique copy per major route** — the top 10-15 routes deserve a paragraph a
   human wrote about that road, not only generated figures.
10. **Performance pass** — Core Web Vitals on real mobile data.
